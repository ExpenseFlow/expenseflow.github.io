Common questions

Which IRS mileage rate do I use in 2026?add

Use 72.5 cents per mile for business driving from January 1 to June 30, 2026, and 76 cents per mile from July 1 to December 31, 2026. The rate depends on the date of each trip.

Can I deduct my commute?add

Generally no. Driving between your home and a regular workplace is commuting. Trips from your workplace or home office to clients, job sites or other business stops can qualify. Ask your tax professional about your situation.

Can employees deduct mileage?add

Under current federal rules, W-2 employees generally cannot deduct unreimbursed mileage. Self-employed people (Schedule C) can use the standard mileage rate. Employers can reimburse at or below the IRS rate under an accountable plan.

Do I need a mileage log?add

Yes. The IRS requires adequate records, such as the date, distance, destination and business purpose of each trip, kept at or near the time of the trip. A year-end estimate is not enough.

Standard mileage or actual expenses?add

The standard rate is simpler: miles × rate. Actual expenses (fuel, insurance, repairs, depreciation) times your business-use percentage can be higher for expensive or low-mileage vehicles. Rules limit switching methods, so check before your first year of use.
