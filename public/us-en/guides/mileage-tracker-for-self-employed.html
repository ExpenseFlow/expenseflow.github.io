Mileage tracker for self-employed drivers: what to look for

Self-employed drivers pay for every mile themselves, which makes missed trips expensive. Here is how to choose a mileage tracker and what to keep for tax time.

Home › Guides › Mileage tracker for self-employed drivers

Why self-employed drivers need a mileage tracker

If you are self-employed, vehicle costs are usually reported on Schedule C, and Part IV of that form asks for your business, commuting and other miles. A running log makes that part of your return a copy-and-paste job instead of a guess. It also protects you if the deduction is questioned.

Who benefits most

  • Rideshare and delivery drivers
  • Real estate agents driving to showings
  • Contractors and tradespeople moving between job sites
  • Consultants and freelancers visiting clients
  • Mobile healthcare and field-service workers

What to look for in a mileage tracker

  • Automatic trip detection so you never forget to start a log
  • Business and personal classification with a purpose on each trip
  • Accurate GPS and visible routes as supporting evidence
  • Receipt capture for tolls, parking and fuel in the same place
  • Exports your accountant can use (PDF and CSV)
  • Battery-friendly tracking that runs in the background

A simple weekly routine

Once a week, open your trip list, mark each drive as business or personal, add a purpose to anything unlabeled, and snap receipts you collected. Five minutes a week beats a weekend of reconstruction in March.
