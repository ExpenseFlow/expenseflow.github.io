What the IRS expects in a mileage log

DateWhen each business trip happened.
DestinationWhere you went.
Business purposeWhy: client visit, supplier run, job site.
MilesDistance of each trip, plus odometer readings or total miles for the year.
Kept at the timeRecords made at or near the time of the trip hold up best.

What the template does

  • Distance fills in from odometer start and end, or type it yourself.
  • The Summary tab totals your miles and works out the deduction at the 72.5¢ and 76¢ 2026 rates, split by date.
  • Business distance by month, and an estimate of the hours you spend logging by hand.
  • Prints cleanly on one page width. Space for 300 trips.

Where a spreadsheet struggles

  • Remembering. Every trip is typed by hand, and most people fall behind after a few weeks.
  • Proof. Cells can be edited later, so a spreadsheet does not show when a trip was recorded.
  • Receipts and odometer photos live somewhere else.

Common questions

What does the IRS require in a mileage log?add

Adequate records: the date, distance, destination and business purpose of each trip, made at or near the time of the trip. Also keep your total miles for the year if you also use the vehicle personally.

Can I use this in Google Sheets?add

Yes. Open Google Sheets, choose File, then Import, then Upload, and select the downloaded file. Formulas work the same way.

Which rates does it use?add

The 2026 IRS standard mileage rates: 72.5 cents for January to June and 76 cents for July to December. Both are editable on the Summary tab.

Is this tax advice?add

No. It is a record-keeping tool. Confirm what you can deduct with your accountant.
