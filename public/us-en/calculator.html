How this is worked out

  • Deduction = business miles × the IRS standard mileage rate: 72.5¢ for Jan 1 – Jun 30 and 76¢ for Jul 1 – Dec 31, 2026.
  • Tax saved = deduction × your tax rate. A deduction is not a refund, so the saving is a fraction of it.
  • The full-year option averages the two rates, assuming half your miles fall in each period.

Good to know

  • Commuting between home and a regular workplace is generally not deductible.
  • This is for self-employed people and business owners. W-2 employees generally cannot deduct unreimbursed mileage.
  • The standard rate is one method. Actual expenses (gas, insurance, repairs, depreciation) can be higher for some vehicles. Ask your accountant.
  • The IRS requires adequate records to support a deduction. A trip log with date, miles, destination and purpose is the usual way.

Common questions

What tax rate should I pick?add

Use the rate on your next dollar of income: your federal bracket plus state tax. Self-employed people also pay about 14% in self-employment tax, so many land between 30% and 40%.

Which IRS rate does it use?add

72.5 cents per mile for driving from January 1 to June 30, 2026, and 76 cents per mile from July 1 to December 31. Choose a period, or use the full-year average.

Can I deduct my commute?add

Generally no. Trips between your home and a regular workplace are commuting. Trips to clients, job sites and other business stops can qualify.

Is this tax advice?add

No. It is an estimate to help you plan. Confirm details with your accountant.
