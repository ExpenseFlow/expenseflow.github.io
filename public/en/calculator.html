How this is worked out

  • Reimbursement: distance × your rate per km or mile.
  • Tax saved: the same deduction × your tax rate. A deduction is not a refund, so the saving is a share of it.
  • Amounts use your own currency. Nothing is converted.

Good to know

  • Commuting between home and a regular workplace is usually not business travel.
  • Rules differ by country. If you are in the US or Canada, use the US calculator or the Canadian calculator, which apply local rates.

Common questions

What rate should I enter?add

Use the rate set by your employer or your local tax authority. For example, the 2026 IRS rate is 72.5 cents per mile (Jan–Jun) and 76 cents (Jul–Dec), and the 2026 CRA rate is 73 cents per km for the first 5,000 km, then 67 cents.

What is the difference between reimbursement and a tax deduction?add

A reimbursement is money your employer pays you back for business driving. A deduction reduces your taxable income, so the real saving is only a share of it, set by your tax rate.

What tax rate should I pick?add

Use the rate you pay on your next unit of income, including any local and regional taxes. If you are unsure, 25% to 30% is a common middle figure.

Is this tax advice?add

No. It is an estimate to help you plan. Confirm details with your employer or a qualified accountant.
