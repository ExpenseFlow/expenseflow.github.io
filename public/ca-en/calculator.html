How this is worked out

  • Self-employed: business-use % = business km ÷ total km. Deduction = vehicle costs × that %. Tax saved = deduction × your tax rate.
  • Employee: an employer can pay a reasonable per-km allowance tax-free. For 2026 the CRA rates are 73¢ for the first 5,000 km and 67¢ after (77¢ and 71¢ in the territories).
  • The estimate ignores capital cost allowance, lease and loan interest limits, and CPP. Your accountant applies those.

Good to know

  • Commuting between home and a regular workplace is generally not business travel.
  • Your logbook is what proves the business-use percentage. The CRA expects the date, destination, purpose and km for each trip.
  • If you are an employee and your employer does not reimburse you, ask your accountant about Forms T2200 and T777.

Common questions

Can self-employed people claim the CRA per-km rate?add

The per-km rate is what an employer can pay an employee tax-free. Self-employed people filing a T2125 generally claim actual vehicle expenses × business-use percentage. That is what the self-employed mode estimates.

What tax rate should I pick?add

Use your combined federal and provincial marginal rate, the tax on your next dollar of income. If you are unsure, 25% to 30% is typical for many small businesses.

Why do I need total km as well as business km?add

Your deduction depends on the share of driving that is for business. That share comes from business km divided by total km, read from your odometer and your logbook.

Is this tax advice?add

No. It is an estimate to help you plan. Confirm details with your accountant or the CRA.
