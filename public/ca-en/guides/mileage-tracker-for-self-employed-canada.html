Mileage tracker for self-employed Canadians: what to look for

Self-employed Canadians fund every kilometre themselves, so missed trips cost real money. Here is how to choose a tracker and what to have ready for tax time.

Home › Guides › Mileage tracker for self-employed Canadians

Why you need a logbook if you are self-employed

Self-employed Canadians report business income and expenses on Form T2125, including motor vehicle expenses. Those claims depend on a logbook that shows business use. A running record turns tax time into a quick summary instead of a reconstruction.

Who benefits most

  • Rideshare and delivery drivers
  • Real estate agents driving to showings
  • Contractors and tradespeople moving between job sites
  • Consultants and freelancers visiting clients
  • Mobile healthcare and field-service workers

What to look for in a mileage tracker

  • Automatic trip detection so you never forget to start a log
  • Business and personal classification with a purpose for each trip
  • Accurate GPS and visible routes as supporting evidence
  • Receipt capture for fuel, parking and tolls in the same place
  • Exports your accountant can use (PDF and CSV)
  • Battery-friendly tracking that runs in the background

A simple weekly routine

Once a week, review your trips, mark each drive as business or personal, add a purpose where it is missing, and capture the receipts you collected. Five minutes weekly beats a weekend of catch-up.

Estimate your claim

Use the CRA mileage deduction calculator to estimate what you could claim, then read CRA logbook requirements to make sure your records hold up.

Frequently asked questions

Where do self-employed Canadians claim vehicle expenses?

On Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities. Your logbook supports the business-use portion of the claim.

Do I need receipts as well as a logbook?

Yes. The logbook shows business use, and receipts support the actual costs such as fuel, insurance and maintenance.
